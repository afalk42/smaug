Eric Hughes' foundational 1993 essay articulating the cypherpunk movement's philosophy on privacy as a fundamental right in the digital age. This seminal work makes the case that privacy is necessary for an open society and outlines the role of cryptography and anonymous systems in protecting individual freedom.

## Key Themes

- **Privacy as a right:** Privacy is necessary for an open society in the electronic age, distinct from secrecy
- **Anonymity systems:** Need for anonymous transaction systems to protect privacy while enabling commerce
- **Cryptography imperative:** Encryption is essential; governments and organizations cannot be trusted to grant privacy
- **Code as activism:** "Cypherpunks write code" - practical implementation of privacy-protecting tools is the solution
- **Open deployment:** Cryptographic systems must be widely distributed and resistant to shutdown

## Key Takeaways

- Privacy is the power to selectively reveal oneself to the world
- Strong cryptography enables individuals to defend their own privacy
- Electronic money and anonymous systems are necessary infrastructure for privacy
- Building code to enable privacy is both technical necessity and political act
- Information expansion is inevitable; encryption and anonymity are the only defense
