MIT physicists observed the first clear evidence that quarks create a wake as they speed through quark-gluon plasma, confirming that the plasma behaves like a liquid. This research, involving scientists Yen-Jie Lee and Krishna Rajagopal, provides new insights into the nature of the primordial matter that existed in the infant universe.

## Key Features

- First clear evidence of quarks creating wakes in quark-gluon plasma
- Confirms liquid-like behavior of quark-gluon plasma
- Studies the primordial conditions of the universe
- Research by MIT physicists Yen-Jie Lee and Krishna Rajagopal
- Advances understanding of the trillion-degree-hot soup that existed in the first moments after the Big Bang

## Key Takeaways

- Quark-gluon plasma behaves like a fluid rather than a gas
- The observation of particle wakes demonstrates collective behavior in extreme conditions
- This behavior has implications for understanding the early universe's properties
- The research connects modern particle physics to cosmology
