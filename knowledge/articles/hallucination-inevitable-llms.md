This paper formalizes and proves that hallucinations are mathematically inevitable in large language models. Rather than focusing on empirical solutions, the authors employ learning theory to demonstrate that LLMs cannot learn all computable functions and will therefore inevitably hallucinate when used as general problem solvers.

## Key Takeaways

- Hallucinations are not just current implementation problems but fundamental theoretical limitations of LLMs
- The paper defines hallucination as inconsistencies between a computable LLM and a computable ground truth function
- Even with constraints on time complexity, certain tasks are inherently hallucination-prone
- This has important implications for safe deployment of LLMs in critical applications
- Existing hallucination mitigators have limited theoretical efficacy
